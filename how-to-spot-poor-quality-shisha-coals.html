How to Spot Poor Quality Shisha Coals Before Buying

Published on October 2026 • Chocolate Shisha Store NG

A clean-looking cube can still hide poor charcoal inside. To learn how to spot poor quality shisha coals, check for loose carbon granules, cracks or crumbling, and excessive smoke or a strong chemical odour when burning. Coconut charcoal with ash content below 2.5% is a useful quality benchmark.

When choosing coconut charcoal briquettes, shape and size matter, but texture and smell can reveal more. This guide explains how to spot poor quality shisha coals by checking the briquette, its burn and the ash it leaves behind. Use these practical signs to compare products before buying.

Key Takeaways
  • Look inside a broken briquette for evenly carbonised material, not loose, pale granules.
  • Treat strong chemical smells or excessive smoke during lighting as warning signs.

Identifying carbon granules in briquettes

Check the briquette’s interior as well as its surface. Its carbonisation and the ash it leaves offer useful clues to coconut charcoal quality.

1. Inspect Briquette Carbon Granules

Examine a broken or chipped edge and look for compact, evenly dark material. Well-carbonised granules can be a sign of thorough carbonisation. Loose, pale or visibly uneven particles can signal incomplete carbonisation, so avoid relying on a smooth exterior alone. Check several briquettes from the pack for consistency.

2. Check Fixed Carbon and Ash Thresholds

Check whether the product provides clear information about its fixed carbon and ash content. High-quality coconut charcoal briquettes are commonly described as having ash content between 1.5% and 2.5%. More ash means more residue after burning, while fixed carbon indicates the carbon-rich portion of the charcoal.

3. Consider Carbonisation and Manufacturing

Carbonisation (also called pyrolysis) turns coconut shells into charcoal through heating in a low-oxygen environment. The material is then ground, mixed with water and binder, shaped, dried and packed. These steps affect density and consistency, so compare the granules across cubes rather than judging one piece.

Chemical odour as a contamination sign

A 25 mm coconut charcoal cube on an electric coil typically reaches an even red in roughly 5–12 minutes. Poor-quality coals can produce excess smoke and combustion gases. Avoid inhaling emissions.

Notice Odours During Ignition

Light a briquette in a well-ventilated area and notice its smoke and smell as it ignites. Coconut charcoal should not produce a persistent odour, smoke or sparks while burning. A sharp chemical smell may point to unwanted additives, unprocessed material or a binder issue.

Check Smoke and Odours During Combustion

Watch the coal as it heats and continues burning. Persistent smoke, a strong chemical odour or sparks are warning signs. If the hookah smoke has an unusual chemical taste, stop using that coal rather than trying to cover the taste with shisha.

Visual signs of poor-quality shisha coals

Spend 30 seconds checking the shape, surface and colour before choosing coconut briquettes. Cracks, crumbling and uneven burning can point to inconsistent manufacturing.

Inspect Coal Appearance

Turn each briquette and inspect its edges, faces and corners. Avoid cubes with deep cracks, loose fragments, soft spots or visibly uneven shapes. A firm, compact surface and consistent size are more useful signs than a polished exterior alone.

Check the Fully Lit Colour

Heat a coconut cube until it glows red, then compare its faces. A patchy glow suggests uneven heating, while an even red glow indicates the coal has heated through. After burning, check the ash: it should form a coherent gray-white layer rather than collapse into loose powder.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the signs of poor-quality shisha coals?

Inconsistent shapes, brittle pieces and a strong smell are reasons for caution. During a session, watch for uneven heating or an unexpected taste. Keep a note of how a new batch performs so you can compare it with a familiar one.

Do poor-quality shisha coals produce excessive smoke, sparks, or odour?

Yes. Excess smoke, sparks or a chemical smell are reasons to stop using the coal. Move it away from the shisha and avoid breathing its emissions. Use coals in a well-ventilated setting.

How do low-quality shisha coals affect flavour and heat?

Uneven or intense heat can scorch tobacco and make its taste bitter or harsh. If the flavour turns unpleasant, remove a coal and let the bowl cool before adjusting the setup.
